Lease vs Buy a Car: How to Compare the True Cost

Dealers like to talk about the monthly payment. But a low payment can hide a long loan, a big down payment or a lease that leaves you with nothing at the end. To compare fairly, look at the true cost per year.

1. Start with the out-the-door price

Ask every dealer for the out-the-door price: the price after discounts, plus dealer fees, add-ons, tax and registration. In the USA and Canada, sales tax or GST/HST is usually added on top; in the UK, VAT is normally included in the price.

2. Add the cost of paying

  • Cash: no interest, but the money is tied up.
  • Finance: total of all monthly payments minus the price is your interest cost.
  • Lease: monthly payments plus fees at signing; at the end you hand the car back.

3. Add running costs

Fuel, insurance and maintenance can differ a lot between cars. Estimate them per year from your mileage.

4. Subtract what the car is worth at the end

If you buy, the car still has a resale value when you sell it. If you lease, it is zero. Then divide the total by the number of years to get a fair cost per year for every offer.
