How to Calculate Unit Price at the Grocery Store (and Save Money)

The bigger pack is not always cheaper, and "2 for $5" is not always a deal. The only fair way to compare is the unit price: how much you pay for each ounce, gram, litre or item.

The unit price formula

Unit price = price ÷ size

Example: a 64 fl oz bottle of milk costs $2.69. 2.69 ÷ 64 = $0.042 per fl oz. A 128 fl oz gallon costs $3.68. 3.68 ÷ 128 = $0.029 per fl oz. The gallon is about 31% cheaper per ounce, even though the price on the shelf is higher.

Always compare the same unit

Only compare ounces with ounces, grams with grams, or items with items. If one pack is in pounds and the other in ounces, convert first (1 lb = 16 oz, 1 kg = 1,000 g, 1 litre = 1,000 ml).

Watch out for these traps

  • Multi-buy offers: divide the deal price by the total size of all packs.
  • Coupons: subtract the coupon from the price before dividing.
  • Food you will not use: a cheaper unit price is only a saving if nothing goes to waste.

Keep a price book

Shoppers who save the most keep a simple "price book": a list of the items they buy often with the best unit price they have seen and where. After a few weeks you will know instantly whether a sale is good.
