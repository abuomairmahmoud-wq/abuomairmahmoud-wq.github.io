How to Tell If a Black Friday Deal Is Real (Free Spreadsheet)

Every November, prices get marked as "50% off" that were never really full price. The good news: you don't need special software to know whether a deal is real. You only need three numbers for each item and a few minutes before the sales start.

1. Write your list before the sales start

Impulse buying is where most holiday money goes. Make a wishlist of what you actually want or need now, while prices are normal. For each item note the store and the regular price you see today.

2. Set a target price for each item

Decide the price at which you would happily buy. A common rule of thumb is 20 to 30% below the regular price for electronics and home goods, but it is your budget, so pick a number you are comfortable with. Writing the target down before the sale stops you from being swayed by big red banners.

3. Track the price you see during the sale

When Black Friday, Cyber Monday or Boxing Day arrives, type in the price you see at each store. Now compare:

  • Price today is at or below your target: this is a real deal for you, buy it.
  • Price today is within about 10% of your target: close, check other stores.
  • Price today is above that: wait. Many items drop again later in the season.

4. Count what you really saved, including tax

Your real saving is the regular price minus what you actually paid. In the USA and most of Canada, sales tax or GST/HST is added at checkout, so include it when you check your budget. In the UK, VAT is already included in the shelf price.
